# Tileset and sprites

A map tile (`TILE_SIZE`) is **64px** in world pixels, but art declares its own pixel size and is drawn one tile big: `TERRAIN_SHEET.tileSize` (32) for the terrain sheet, and `frame` on every entry of `SPRITES` in `src/assets/sprites.ts` (32 for the old art, 64 for `Slime_01` and `Warrior_02`). The old 32px art is therefore shown at 2x until it's replaced; new art is 64px (see the 64px plan, and `/mnt/project-files/art/sprite-spec.md`). `src/assets/sprites.test.ts` checks every image's real size against what it declares. Sizes in code-drawn pieces (shadows, the outline, rank badges) are fractions of a tile, and effect sizes tuned on the 32px grid are multiplied by `GRID_PX`. The art comes in two forms:

Straights and rounded corners come from the pack's pink rounded-square loop (a 3x3 of path tiles around a gem), and the heads from the tiles just below it. ## Units: idle and move sheets

**[units-catalog.md](units-catalog.md)** lists every unit sheet in `src/assets/units/` (the whole pack: 16 units, six team-color/race variants each) with what it shows, its footprint, and what to check before using it. Read it before picking unit art.

Each unit art (`UNIT_SPRITES`, e.g. `Soldier_03`) has one sheet per animation in `src/assets/units/`, named `{Unit}_{NN}_{Animation}.png` after the source pack: `_Idle` and `_Move`. The texture key is the file name without `.png`, built by `unitSheetKey(art, animation)`. Every pack sheet in the folder is already at 2x (`Warrior_02` and `Amazon_01` are true 32px art). Both are 128x128 sheets of 4x4 32px frames with the same layout. Each row is a facing direction, each column an animation frame (idle: a small bob; move: a walk cycle, with the body shifted 1–2px and the feet alternating). Rows, top to bottom: **down** (toward the camera), **left** (west), **right** (east), **up** (back to the camera). Left vs right was read from the pixels: in row 1 the face, the single visible eye and the soldier's visor sit on the left, the hat's tip / ears trail to the right, and the soldier's spear points lower left; row 2 mirrors it. The source PNGs are RGB with a `tRNS` color key making black transparent. Feet: the villager's bottom outline is x 10–21, y 28–29; the soldier's is wider (a spear sticks out lower left) down to y 31.

`GridScene` loads every sprite in `SPRITES` as a spritesheet of its declared frame size and plays `<sheet key>-<facing>` on each unit (e.g. `Warrior_02_Idle-down`), keeping the unit's art and facing in the sprite's data. Units start out facing `defaultFacing` (down); `walkSprite` plays the move animation facing each step (`getPathFacings` in `src/game/facing.ts`), then idles facing the last step. The React `UnitSprite` crops the default facing's row of the idle sheet out of the sheet with a CSS background, showing its first frame, or with `animated` looping the same idle frames as the map (the deployment roster uses this).

## Units: single images (generated art)

A unit can instead be **one image** (`SINGLE_IMAGE_UNITS` in `tileset.ts`): the character facing right on a transparent square, mirrored to face left, animated in code by `src/game/unitRig.ts` / `src/scenes/unitRig.ts` (breathing, a hop per step, an attack lean, a hit recoil). This is the path for image-generator art, which can't keep frames consistent. To add one:

1. Prep the picture: `python3 .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/prep-unit-image.py in.png src/assets/units/Knight.png [--flip] [--key-background]`. It clears faint alpha noise (which would box the outline), optionally keys out a flat background from the corners, crops, mirrors left-facing art (`--flip`), and fits it on a 128px square (`--size`) with the feet at 30/32 down, where `UNIT_SHADOW` sits.
2. Import it in `sprites.ts` as `Knight: { url: knight, frame: 128, smooth: true }` (`smooth` for painted art: Phaser filters it linearly and the outline matches; leave it off for pixel art).
3. Add `'Knight'` to `SINGLE_IMAGE_UNITS` and map the class to it in `UNIT_SPRITES`. `getUnitTextureKey` then draws it from the image for every animation, and the React `UnitSprite` shows the whole image (breathing in CSS when `animated`).

Player and guest units start facing right, enemies left; they turn toward each step and toward whoever they fight.

## Trees: gold ginkgo sheet

`gold_ginkgo.png` is 131x131: 16 variants of a golden ginkgo tree in a 4x4 grid of 32px frames with a **1px transparent gutter** between them (frame origins at 0, 33, 66, 99); the game doesn't load it (Phaser would need `spacing: 1`). Each frame is one whole tree: a yellow-gold canopy (`#fdd633` / `#dd980f`, outlined `#31161c`) over a dark brown trunk, standing on its own ellipse of grass (`#a6ce5d` with a `#6f9e43` rim) that fills the bottom ~10 rows. Content spans x 1–30, y 0–31 in rows 0–2; the bottom row's frames stop at y 29 (the sheet is cut off, so their grass ellipse is missing its last 2 rows). Some variants have extras on the base: variant 9 has a small pond (`#638ea7` / `#c1dce1`) at lower right, variant 14's trunk has a dark root/branch reaching right. `gold_ginkgo_tree.png` and `ginkgo_tree_green.png` are the same drawing in two colorways, made to match the terrain sheet: **16px art doubled to 32x32** (every art pixel is a 2x2 block), three flat sheet colors each, the dark leaf tone doubling as the outline. The canopy is a heart-shaped fan like a ginkgo leaf — two lobes with a notch at the top and a dark center vein — spanning x 0–31, y 2–23, with a few dark leaf dots; the trunk (`#8f4d57`, the pine tile's trunk brown) is x 14–17, y 22–27 with a foot at x 12–19, y 28–29, and rows 0–1 and 30–31 are empty. Green uses the pine forest's `#3b7d4f` / `#84b14d`; gold uses the sheet's amber pair `#cf752b` / `#f0b541`. Neither has a grass patch, so each is drawn over `TREE_SHADOW`, a translucent ellipse at its foot. (They replaced a true-32px, ~28-color painterly ginkgo that didn't match the sheet.) `green_ginkgo_tree.png` is a separate 4x4, 1px-gutter sheet of green trees (same layout as `gold_ginkgo.png`, bottom-row frames run y 1–30) that the game doesn't load. The demo level places the gold tree and its green recolor at `TREE_POSITIONS` in `demoLevel.ts`; `GridScene.renderDecorations` draws them over the grass at depth 0.4, each over its shadow (`addTreeShadow` in `src/scenes/unitShadow.ts`, depth 0.35), and they don't block movement.

## Item icons: src/assets/items/

One 32x32 PNG per item, named by item id, painted at a true 32x32 (not 16px doubled), transparent background, no shadow, alpha snapped to fully on or off. Generated with PixelLab: `create_image_pro_flash` at 32x32, `max_colors: 16`, the old 16px-doubled `iron-axe` as the style image for outline and shading only; prompt: the subject plus "single centered object filling most of the canvas, detailed GBA-era 16-bit RPG item icon, single color dark outline, rich shading with highlights, no shadow, no background" (weapons "lying diagonally with the business end at the upper right"). Each keeps its own colors. Weak spots: `wooden-spear` is very thin, `wooden-sword` and `club` sit small on the canvas, `bite`'s jaws read loosely, and `ballista` is drawn as a wheeled catapult. The raw outputs and `prompts.txt` are on the art machine under `pixellab/rogue-emblem-icons32/`. `src/game/tileset.test.ts` fails when a run item or weapon has no icon.

## Reward icons: src/assets/rewards/

One 32x32 PNG per reward kind whose card doesn't show an item or unit (`draft`, `rest`, `relic`, `gold`, `lesson`, `scroll`), named by kind, made with the item icon recipe above: a stack of ribbon-tied contracts with a wax seal, a campfire, a gold amulet with a purple gem, a heap of gold coins, an open book under a glowing blue star, a sealed scroll. Weak spot: `gold` is a lumpy heap that only partly reads as coins. Only the reward screen shows them (`REWARD_ICON_URLS` in `src/assets/rewardIcons.ts`, a Vite glob); `src/assets/rewardIcons.test.ts` checks the set.

## Glyph icons: src/assets/glyphs/

16x16 PNGs, transparent, one per `GlyphKind` and per `StatIconKind` except `health` (named by kind, e.g. `crit.png`, `rout.png`), painted with PixelLab in the item icons' style (dark outline, a few shades). Shown at 1x (16 CSS px), 12px in the reward tiles' EXP and the merchant's prices (smoothed there, since pixelated 0.75x drops rows). `glyphIcons.test.ts` checks every kind has one and each is 16x16. `range.png` (a dotted arrow) and `book.png` are the weakest and the first to repaint.

## Boon icons: src/assets/boons/

One 32x32 PNG per level-up boon (`BOONS` in `src/game/warband/boons.ts`), named by boon id (`stat-health`, `skill-cleave`, `keen-eye`, ...): 16px art doubled, transparent background, no shadow, alpha snapped to fully on or off. Generated with PixelLab (`create_image_pro_flash` at 16x16, `max_colors: 12`, the wooden sword halved to 16px as the style image for outline, shading and detail only, not colors; prompt: the subject plus "single centered object filling most of the canvas, chunky GBA-era 16-bit RPG item icon, single color dark outline, basic shading, low detail, no shadow, limited palette"), then upscaled 2x. Unlike the wooden items they keep their own colors (each is its subject's color: a red heart, a green clover, a blue crystal...), and with `max_colors` set they need no palette snapping. Stat boons show their stat's symbol (heart, flexed arm, tome, bullseye, winged boot, clover, kite shield, amulet), skill boons the weapon or motion, passives a symbol of the effect. Weak spots: brawler's gauntlet spikes barely show, quick-study is a plain scroll (its star didn't come out) and regeneration's plus is a yellow speck. Only React shows them (`BOON_ICON_URLS` in `src/assets/boonIcons.ts`, a Vite glob, not `SPRITE_URLS`); `src/assets/boonIcons.test.ts` fails when a boon has no icon or an icon has no boon. The raw PixelLab outputs and their prompts are in the project files under `art/boon-icons/raw/`.

Parchment fill is `#f0d9a8`, banner blue `#2a4fa8`, banner red `#a82a2a`, banner green `#2a8034` (the red banner with its three reds swapped for greens: outline-side dark `#0e3214`, fill, bevel `#62ba5a`).

## Gate: gates.png

`src/assets/gates.png` is 96x64 (3x2 tiles): one red Chinese-style paifang gate with a tiered roof and a gold plaque in the middle, drawn within x 3–85, y 3–56 (transparent margin around it). It was the top-left cell of a 192x128 sheet of four gate variants (a 2x2 grid of 96x64 cells), cropped down at the user's request. Its posts stand at x ~10–13, 27–30, 58–61 and 75–78, so the opening under the plaque is the middle tile column (x 31–57). The game draws it as `STRUCTURE_SPRITES.gate` (texture key `gates`, 3x2 tiles): Like every sprite it loads as a spritesheet of its declared frame size (32), so its six tiles are frames 0–5 in reading order. `GridScene.renderStructures` draws it tile by tile from `getStructureTiles` (`src/game/structures.ts`): the top `roofRows` row (the roof and plaque) at depth 0.8, over units (0.75) and under the cursor (1), so a unit in the opening's top tile stands behind the roof (only its hat tip shows above it); the bottom row (the posts) at the trees' depth 0.4, under units. Level 1 (`demoLevel.ts`) puts it at the north end of the dirt path; all six of its tiles are walkable, so units can stand behind the roof or by the posts on any column.

## Terrain: overworld sheet

**[overworld-catalog.md](overworld-catalog.md)** lists the map art on the sheet: grass variants, the water, sand, dark-grass and deep-water sets, island rings, mountains, forests, bridges, team-colored buildings and walls. Read it before picking any terrain art. The UI pieces on the same sheet (cursor, movement arrow, digits, HP bar, markers) are in **[ui-catalog.md](ui-catalog.md)**, kept separate so they're never mistaken for map tiles.

The sheet comes from a pack drawn at 3x (every art pixel is a 3x3 block, so it's natively 1024x1024, 16px tiles). `scripts/rescale-png.js` redraws the used area at 2x as `src/assets/overworld.png` (see the catalog for the command), so tile `[column, row]` on the pack is 32px tile `[column, row]` in the game, frame `row * 42 + column`.

**The ground (grass, dirt, river) is no longer drawn from this sheet**: see "Terrain: 64px corner tilesets" below. The sheet's blob autotiles still draw walls (`getWallAutotile`), and its buildings, gate and ford art are still in use.

Blob autotiling works on the map's own cells (not a dual grid): each set in `autotile.ts` is drawn over the ground, with its border inside its own edge cells. Every set uses the same 7-column layout (strip, notched 3x3, plain 3x3), which the catalog diagrams. Water, sand beach and deep water come in **6 animation frames** laid 7 columns apart. The peach sand-patch set (the old dirt path) only has a _notched_ 3x3, so it's only clean one tile wide.

## Terrain: 64px corner tilesets

`src/assets/terrain64.png` (1024x128, `WANG_SHEET`) is the ground: PixelLab `create_topdown_tileset` sets in pro mode (the only mode with 64px tiles, 25 generations a set; it takes no style image, but does take a `palette`, here 42 colors from the concept painting), chained on one grass base tile so both sets' full-grass tile is pixel-identical. Row 0 is grass (lower) to dirt (upper); row 1 water (lower) to grass (upper) with a dark brown rocky cut bank. A grass-to-rock set (gravel under mountains) was tried and dropped: its edges read as cut off, and the 2x2 mountains carry a range on their own; the attempts are kept in the export (`grass_rock`, `grass_rock_plateau/`). Each row holds its 16 tiles **in index order**, `index = NW*8 + NE*4 + SW*2 + SE*1` with a corner 1 when it's the upper terrain, so tile 0 of row 0 is plain grass, 15 plain dirt; 0 of row 1 open water, 15 grass. `src/game/wangTiles.ts` draws them on the map's cells as quarters (`WANG_SETS`: the dirt set is the base under every cell; the water set only where a quarter touches water, bridges or fords). To chain a new set onto the same grass, pass PixelLab `lower_base_tile_id=4ba173ac-1f20-4e43-ad3a-5428538d6b34`.

`src/assets/terrain64-props.png` (512x448, `TERRAIN64_PROPS_SHEET`, 8 columns, 64px, transparent, alpha 0 or 255, no shadows) from one `create_1_direction_object` batch styled with mosaics cut from the concept. Row 0: pine, pine cluster, broadleaf (`FOREST_ART`), mountain peak, twin peaks (`MOUNTAIN_ART.tiles`, with the low ridges and crag in row 2), bridge west–east, bridge north–south (`BRIDGE_ART`), boulders. The bridges are redrawn by `build-terrain64.js` from PixelLab's north–south bridge (its own spans didn't join, and its 29px deck was too narrow for a 64px unit): its rails moved out to columns 2–7 and 57–61 with the planking mirrored across between them, then 32 rows of that deck plus the same rows flipped make a deck that repeats along its length, turned a quarter for west–east; row 2 holds the ends (north, south, west, east), each the deck's half nearest the bridge with a dark lip at its far edge, drawn on the bank cell a bridge runs onto so the deck covers the cut bank. Row 2 columns 4–6: two low ridges and a crag (greyer and smaller than the peaks, so they read as foothills). Rows 3–4: two 128px mountains cut into their four tiles, a massif at `[0, 3]` and a tall peak at `[2, 3]` (`MOUNTAIN_ART.big`); `getFeatureSprites` stands one on each 2x2 block of mountain cells it finds in reading order and gives the other mountain cells one-tile variants. Rows 5–6: three 128px forest clumps, pines `[0, 5]`, mixed `[2, 5]` and broadleaf `[4, 5]` (`FOREST_ART.big`), placed the same way on 2x2 blocks of forest cells; single forest cells keep the row 0 trees. Clumps butted side by side show a thin grass seam between them. Row 1: red, blue and orange roofed houses, watchtower, castle wall (not seamless end to end), round castle tower, gatehouse, village wall, not used yet (buildings still come from the overworld sheet in their palettes). Weak spots: the castle pieces are house-sized, the water's foam is short flecks, and there's no meadow, cliff or waterfall set yet.

Both are built from the PixelLab export in the project files (`art/terrain-64b/out/`: `tilesets/<set>/{sheet.png,wang_map.json}`, `props/<name>.png`, `notes.md` with every setting, id and prompt) by `node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/build-terrain64.js <dir>/out`, which checks every tile's corners against its index.

## Inspecting art

Images this small are unreadable when viewed directly — dump them as ASCII:

```sh
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/dump-png.js src/assets/units/Villager_01_Idle.png src/assets/units/Soldier_03_Idle.png
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/dump-png.js --tile 32 src/assets/overworld.png:0     # one tile of a sheet
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/dump-png.js --colors src/assets/units/Villager_01_Idle.png                # colors + pixel counts
```

Each distinct color gets its own character (most common first), with a `char=hex` legend under the image; transparent pixels are spaces. Characters are assigned per image, so the same character can mean different colors in two dumps.

To _look_ at a block of a sheet (e.g. when cataloging new tiles), render it as a zoomed contact sheet and view the PNG; it labels column/row numbers and prints each tile's opaque bounding box. To check how overlays stack, compose tiles into a little scene (`/` separates rows, spaces cells, `+` layers bottom to top):

```sh
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/sheet-grid.js --scale 3 src/assets/overworld.png <scratchpad>/grid.png 0 0 12 2   # columns 0-12, rows 0-2
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/compose-tiles.js <scratchpad>/scene.png "0,0+4,0 0,0 / 0,0+4,1 5,9+12,1"
```

To judge an autotile set as the game will draw it, `autotile-preview.ts` runs a little `#`/`.` map through `src/game/autotile.ts` over grass (`--over c,r=X` stamps a sheet tile on cells marked X):

```sh
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/autotile-preview.ts --block 1,33 --inner 2,34 --over 5,34=G <scratchpad>/walls.png "......./.##G##./.#...#./.#####./......."
```

To judge the procedural map generator's output, `map-preview.ts` renders `generateMap` maps for some seeds side by side, drawn the way GridScene draws them (terrain, walls, crossings, forests, mountains and buildings in one palette; no ginkgos). `--archetype <type>` picks a map type (`mapArchetypes.ts`), `--terrain '<json>'` sets other terrain options, and `--units N` stands N warriors on the deployment zone (tinted) and N skeletons on enemy spots:

```sh
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/map-preview.ts --scale 1 --palette a-stone <scratchpad>/maps.png 1 2 3
```

`realm-preview.ts` does the same for the Warband overworld (`generateRealm` in `src/game/warband/realm/`): it renders whole realms for some seeds the way the overworld screen paints them (the parchment map from `getRealmInk` and `paintRealmInk`, with each site's icon stood on its tile), one under the other, and prints each realm's sites (id, kind, biome, region) and the names lettered on it:

```sh
node .claude/skills/tileset/scripts/realm-preview.ts <scratchpad>/realms.png 1 2
```

`scripts/rescale-png.js --from N --to M [--crop WxH] in.png out.png` changes the scale of upscaled pixel art (it checks every NxN block is one color). `dump-png.js` exports `decode` and `encode` (rows of RGBA pixels to and from PNG) for other scripts.

## Overworld art: src/assets/realm/

The Warband overworld is a parchment map (`realmInk.ts`, `inkRaster.ts`), not the terrain sheet, so its art is its own. All of it was made with PixelLab `create_image_pro_flash`, transparent background, no style image; the raw outputs and every prompt are in the project files under `art/overworld-ink/raw/`.

- `sites/<SiteKind>.png` (64x64, one per kind: home, battle, village, fort, elite, town, shrine, event, hold): a Tactics Ogre-style location icon, a little diorama on a round raised disc of earth and grass seen three-quarters from above, fully colored (green grass, grey stone, red roofs), single dark brown outline. Opaque bounds run about x 2–62 and y 3–61, the rim's foot at y 56–61; the hold fills the canvas top to bottom (banner tips at y 0, foot at y 63). Home is two canvas tents and a campfire, battle crossed swords and a broken palisade, village thatched cottages round a well, fort a square crenellated stone fort, elite a cave mouth under a horned skull with red eyes, town a walled town with a spire and windmill, shrine a white pillared shrine with a blue crystal, event a signpost crossroads with a covered cart, hold a dark castle with red banners. The screen draws them at two tiles, centered on the site's tile with the disc's foot a little below its middle.

## Map props: map-props.png

`src/assets/map-props.png` (352x64, `MAP_PROPS_SHEET`, 11 columns) holds the crossings the overworld sheet lacks, drawn by `scripts/draw-map-props.js` (run it from the repo root; it rewrites the file) as 16px art doubled, in colors sampled off the sheet. `[i, 0]` and `[i, 1]` are the gateway for building palette `i` (`GATE_ART`, in the script's `GATE_PALETTES` order: a-stone, a-orange, a-teal, a-pink, a-brown, b-white, b-red, b-blue, b-green, b-orange): row 0 is the posts (two 5px piers in the rampart's colors, the arch's dark underside, a sand floor so the dirt road runs through, drawn under units) and row 1 the crenellated lintel (drawn over units). `[10, 0]` (`FORD_ART`) is a ford's two staggered stepping stones in the rocks tile's colors with a pale wake, kept to the tile's middle rows so they clear both banks. `[10, 1]` (`SPIKES_ART`) is a spike trap (`getHazardSprites`, on `spikes` cells, under units): five steel spikes in two staggered rows, in the bridge tile `[11, 2]`'s stone colors (sorted darkest first: outline, shaded face, light face, and the brightest as a glint on each tip), standing in a sunken pit of the dirt path's sand with a steel rim, x 2–29, y 12–29, so grass shows round it. ## How the code uses the art

- **`src/game/tileset.ts`** — the only place art is chosen. Everything else refers to art by name (terrain, unit class, UI element, arrow piece). `TERRAIN_SHEET` (texture key and size), `WANG_SHEET` and `WANG_SETS` (the 64px ground), and `TERRAIN64_PROPS_SHEET` with `FOREST_ART`, `MOUNTAIN_ART` and `BRIDGE_ART` place terrain art as `[column, row]` tiles. `CURSOR_ANIMATION` lists the cursor's two sheet tiles and how long each shows, and `ARROW_TILES` gives each arrow piece its sheet tile. `UNIT_SPRITES` (by unit class, via `getUnitSprite(unitClass)` with a `DEFAULT_UNIT_SPRITE` fallback) names each class's **unit art** — a unit looks the same on either team, `unitSheetKey(art, animation)` turns that into the texture key of one of its sheets, `UNIT_SHEET` says how unit sheets are cut (a row per facing) and `UNIT_ANIMATIONS` how each animation is timed. `FOREST_ART` and `MOUNTAIN_ART` list the overlay variants for forest and mountain cells, `BUILDING_ART` places each building kind (`src/game/buildings.ts`), and `BUILDING_PALETTES` names the ten building colors (set A's light gray is called `a-stone`). A level draws every building and wall in one palette, so colors never mix. Never put frame numbers or keys directly in scenes or logic.
- **`src/game/mapArt.ts`** — pure picking of one-tile overlays from the sheet: `getFeatureSprites` (forests and mountains, with the mountain stacking rule from the catalog), `getBuildingSprites` (buildings, tall tops and flags in a palette) and `getWallAutotile` (a palette's rampart set). Each sprite has a layer: `ground` (under units), `cap` (art poking into the cell north) or `roof` (over units).
- **`src/assets/sprites.ts`** — `SPRITE_URLS`, texture key → imported image URL for every standalone sprite. GridScene preloads each under its key; the React `UnitSprite` component shows one by key. A new sprite means importing its file here and naming its key in `tileset.ts`. `tileset.test.ts` checks every key in `tileset.ts` has a URL.
- **`src/game/autotile.ts`** — pure autotile logic. Each terrain cell is drawn as four quarter tiles; `getQuarterShapes(grid, x, y, terrain)` names each quarter's shape from its two side neighbors and the diagonal between them (`'outer'`, `'horizontal'`, `'vertical'`, `'inner'`, `'full'`). That covers all 47 blob combinations. Cells off the map take the nearest cell on it, so terrain continues past the edge. `getShapeTile` picks which sheet tile's same quarter draws a shape, and `getQuarterFrames` turns that into frame numbers on the sheet cut into 16px quarters (2 x 42 per row), shifted by `columnStride` for each animation frame.
- **`GridScene`** — `renderTerrain` lays one 32px-quarter tilemap per Wang set (`getWangFrameMap`), then one 16px-tile layer per autotiled terrain (walls) (each layer scaled up by `TILE_SIZE / TERRAIN_SHEET.tileSize`) from `getQuarterFrames` (`wall` in the level's palette), precomputing every animation frame and stepping the layer's tile indices on a timer. `renderMapSprites` draws `mapArt.ts`'s overlays as sheet frames. `addTileSprite(x, y, key)` draws a sprite stretched to exactly one tile, so 16px and 32px images both fit. Every unit is outlined in its team's color by `addUnitOutline` (`src/scenes/unitOutline.ts`; colors and width are `UNIT_OUTLINE` in `tileset.ts`), a white silhouette ring built per sheet from the art's alpha (`getOutlineMask`, 1 sheet pixel wide, cut at frame edges) and tinted. The unit sprites have no shadows of their own, so `addUnitShadow` (`src/scenes/unitShadow.ts`) puts a translucent pixel ellipse under each unit's feet. Its size, position and opacity are `UNIT_SHADOW` in `tileset.ts`, set for where the units' feet touch the ground (see Units above), so retune it if new unit art stands differently. GridScene loads the overworld sheet as a 32px spritesheet, so the tilemaps use it whole and the cursor and arrow sprites draw single frames of it. Bosses and elites (`Unit.rank`) also get a badge from `addRankBadge` (`src/scenes/rankBadge.ts`): `RANK_BADGES` in `tileset.ts`, 11x11 pixel art written as rows of palette letters (boss: a white skull on a red plate; elite: a gold star on a violet plate, both outlined `#1a0d12`), drawn at 1 world pixel per art pixel (finer than the 2x unit art, so it reads as a HUD mark) at tile pixel (21, 0), over the art's top-right corner. The React `RankBadge` draws the same rows as SVG. No art-choice rules in the scene.

## Rules when changing art

1. If you decode something about an image that isn't written down here (a new sheet's layout, what a sprite shows), add it to this file so it isn't re-derived.
2. Keep frame numbers and sprite keys in `tileset.ts`, and image imports in `sprites.ts`. Keep tile-selection rules as pure functions in `src/game/` with Vitest tests (see `autotile.test.ts`): test shape names, plus one test pinning the actual frame numbers.
3. New map art should be 64x64 per tile (declare `frame: 64` in `sprites.ts`); a unit sheet is then 256x256. `scripts/assemble-unit-sheet.js <stillsDir> <Unit_NN>` builds a unit's Idle and Move sheets from four finished stills of any size.
4. An autotile set only borders its own cells against _anything else_, so any number of terrains can each be a layer over grass. Two sets that meet directly (e.g. sand next to water) just butt their borders together.
5. To preview terrain art without a browser, compose tiles with `decode` from `scripts/dump-png.js` and the pure `autotile.ts` functions into a PNG, and view that.
